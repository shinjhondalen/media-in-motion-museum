01 / EXPLORE THE CHANNELS

ONE WORLD.
DIFFERENT SIGNALS.

Each medium has its own strengths and limitations. Knowing the difference helps us choose where to look, what to trust, and how to respond.

CHANNEL 01▤

PRINT MEDIA

Physical text and images created for reading, reference, and distribution.

NewspapersBooksMagazinesFlyers
STRENGTH Easy to keep, annotate, and revisit.
LIMITATION Information may become outdated.
CHANNEL 02▣

BROADCAST MEDIA

Audio and video distributed to audiences through established channels.

RadioTelevisionPublic service ads
STRENGTH Reaches many people through sound and visuals.
LIMITATION Editing can influence emphasis and interpretation.
CHANNEL 03⌘

DIGITAL MEDIA

Information created, stored, or shared using electronic devices and platforms.

WebsitesE-booksEmailDatabases
STRENGTH Searchable, accessible, and easy to update.
LIMITATION Errors can be copied and spread quickly.
CHANNEL 04◎

NEW MEDIA

Interactive networked platforms where audiences can also create and respond.

Social mediaPodcastsLivestreams
STRENGTH Encourages participation and community voices.
LIMITATION Virality does not guarantee truth.
CHANNEL 05▶

FILM MEDIA

Stories and information presented through moving images, editing, and sound.

MoviesDocumentariesExplainer videos
STRENGTH Makes complex ideas engaging and visual.
LIMITATION Editing may leave out important context.
CHANNEL 06⌖

OUTDOOR MEDIA

Messages displayed in public spaces to reach people in their surroundings.

BillboardsPostersBanners
STRENGTH Visible to people in everyday public spaces.
LIMITATION Limited room for details and context.

02 / APPLY THE IDEAS

THE MESSAGE
THAT WENT VIRAL.

One announcement. Six channels. Different ways people might encounter the same information.

ORIGINAL CLASSROOM SCENARIO / FICTIONAL

A “FREE LAPTOP GRANT” is circulating among students.

A forwarded post claims that every student can receive a free laptop if they register immediately, submit their school ID, and pay a small delivery fee. The message has no named sponsor or official application page.

▤ PRINT

A school bulletin board displays a printed notice. Students should check whether the school administration posted or approved it.

▣ BROADCAST

A radio or television report may discuss the grant. Listeners should check whether the station identifies a credible sponsoring organization.

⌘ DIGITAL

A search result leads to a website. Students should inspect the web address, contact details, privacy policy, and official announcements.

◎ NEW MEDIA

The post spreads in group chats and social feeds. Shares and comments are not proof; look for the original source.

▶ FILM

A short promotional video shows students receiving laptops. Viewers should ask who produced it and whether the footage supports the actual claim.

⌖ OUTDOOR

A poster appears near campus. Students should confirm the sponsor with the school before scanning a QR code or submitting information.

RED FLAGS DETECTEDUrgent deadline · Request for school ID · Delivery fee · No official sponsor · No verifiable application page

03 / THINK CRITICALLY

RUN THE
R.A.V.A.T. CHECK.

Before believing or sharing a claim, evaluate the source. These five questions can help you slow down and examine information more carefully.

R

RELIABILITY

Is the source known for providing trustworthy information? Does it have a record of responsible reporting?

A

ACCURACY

Can the names, dates, figures, and claims be confirmed through evidence and other credible sources?

V

VALUE

Is the information useful, relevant, and meaningful for understanding an issue or making a decision?

A

AUTHORITY

Who created the content? Does the author or organization have relevant knowledge or credentials?

T

TIMELINESS

When was it published or updated? Is it still current for the topic being discussed?

REMEMBER: Popularity is not proof. Pause → Investigate → Confirm → Share responsibly.

04 / ACT RESPONSIBLY

THE 10 RULES
OF NETIQUETTE.

Virginia Shea's rules remind us that good digital citizenship begins with respect, empathy, and responsibility.

01

Remember the human.

Treat people online with respect and empathy.

02

Follow the same standards as real life.

Be honest and respectful in digital spaces.

03

Know where you are in cyberspace.

Understand the purpose and rules of each online community.

04

Respect people's time and bandwidth.

Keep messages relevant and avoid unnecessary spam.

05

Make yourself look good online.

Communicate clearly and think about your digital footprint.

06

Share expert knowledge.

Contribute useful information and cite your sources.

07

Keep flame wars under control.

Disagree with ideas without insulting people.

08

Respect other people's privacy.

Ask permission before sharing personal content.

09

Do not abuse your power.

Never use access or influence to threaten or exploit others.

10

Be forgiving of people's mistakes.

Correct errors calmly and give others room to learn.

Based on Virginia Shea's *Netiquette* (1994).
